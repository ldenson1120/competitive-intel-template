# Competitive Intel: Context

The competitive intel digest reads this file before every run. Edit it whenever your market, competitors or priorities change.

- **How we win:** <the two or three things you claim that competitors can't>
- **How we usually lose:** <for example "a big platform bundles us in for free">

## Competitors

List every competitor in one tier. The digest picks up every file in `Baselines/`, so to add a competitor, add it here and create its baseline.

**Large tier: filtered.** Big platforms that ship constantly, where most news has nothing to do with you.
- <Competitor A>, <Competitor B>

Keep only:
- launches and deprecations in <your category>
- pricing or packaging changes that touch <your category>
- AI or positioning moves aimed at <your buyers>
- acquisitions or partnerships in <your category>

Drop everything else.

**Direct tier: everything notable.** Head-to-head competitors.
- <Competitor C>, <Competitor D>

In scope: launches, pricing, packaging, positioning and homepage changes, leadership and ownership changes, notable hiring (for example a wave of sales roles in a new region), funding, layoffs, and customer wins they publicize.

**Up-and-comers: everything notable, plus traction.** Newer or open-source players that aren't a deal threat yet.
- <Competitor E>

In scope: everything in the direct tier, plus traction signals (funding, a big jump in GitHub stars or contributors, a front-page Hacker News or Reddit launch, a named customer), a first paid offering or any pricing change, and features that overlap your main claims. Report a traction change only when it's big enough to notice, for example up 25% or more since the last recorded figure.

**Per-competitor notes** (optional): <ownership changes, rebrands, other names to search under, what to ignore>

## Customer voice

All public:
- Reddit and Hacker News threads, found through web search
- G2, Capterra or other review sites for your category
- GitHub issues on competitors' public repos
- <any forum, community or subreddit specific to your category>

Look for complaints about: <pricing and overages, missing features, reliability, support, whatever your buyers care about>. Praise only counts if it's for something you claim as your advantage.

## The so-what bar

An item is **worth acting on** only if it changes:
- a message or claim we make
- a campaign: <list campaign names or folders the digest should protect>
- a sales conversation
- a pricing argument

If someone in your buyer's role would read it as normal, it isn't an item. It's at most a logged change. Flag no more than three per run.

## Ground rules

- Never extrapolate. Every item needs a source URL and a date. If it can't be verified, it doesn't go in.
- A competitor's claim is reported as their claim, never as fact.
- Quote at most 15 words from any source.
- Suggested copy follows our brand rules: <for example "never name competitors in public copy">
